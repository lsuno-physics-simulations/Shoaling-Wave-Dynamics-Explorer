Model, interpretation, and classroom prompts

This browser adaptation uses the MATLAB domain (320 × 220 m), bathymetry, breaking threshold H/h > 0.78, and 0.01 s time step. The resolution is reduced for browser performance. The 3D surface height is exaggerated and colored by η², as in MATLAB Simulator 2. The separate lower panel always shows η² over bathymetry contours. White on the upper plot indicates active breaking.

The wave equation is ηtt = g ∇·(h∇η), with an optional heuristic correction −0.01 g h² ∇⁴η, damping, and a prescribed incoming swell. Fixed seeded wave components give coherent directional swell. Disabling directional swell produces a single wave. These replace the original per-step random forcing and nonstandard dispersion expression. This is an educational prototype, not a validated Boussinesq or coastal engineering model.

Energy is displayed as η² (m²), matching the MATLAB proxy; it is not total physical wave energy density. Sediment evolution follows ht = 10⁻⁵∇²(η²), with depth limits of 1–25 m. It is a qualitative proxy without sediment conservation, currents, wetting/drying, or calibrated transport. Depth changes at the default rate are very small.

Try: compare refraction with and without the channel; identify the reef's wave shadow; turn off the sponge and observe reflected waves; increase amplitude and locate breaking. Use the depth map to connect each pattern to the seabed.

No installation, accounts, external libraries, tracking, or network connection is needed after download. Numerical changes from the MATLAB source are documented in README.md.
